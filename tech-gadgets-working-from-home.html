Best Tech Gadgets for Working From Home

Practical technology and accessories that can make your home office more comfortable, organized and productive.

Last updated: October 2026

Working from home can be convenient, but the quality of your workspace can have a major effect on how comfortable and productive your day feels.

You do not need an expensive desk full of technology. The right accessories can solve common problems such as poor posture, limited laptop ports, uncomfortable typing, weak video quality and messy cables.

In this guide, we look at practical gadgets that can help improve a home-office setup without focusing on unnecessary technology.

Best Tech Gadgets for a Home Office

Display

1. External Monitor

An external monitor can make a major difference when your main computer is a laptop. A larger screen gives you more space for documents, spreadsheets, browser windows and communication tools.

A second screen can also reduce the need to constantly switch between applications.

Best for: Multitasking, spreadsheets, research and office work.

Ergonomics

2. Laptop Stand

A laptop stand raises the screen closer to eye level and can help create a more comfortable desk setup.

If you use a separate keyboard and mouse, a laptop stand can allow the laptop to function more like a desktop workstation.

Best for: Laptop users who want a better desk setup.

Productivity

3. Wireless Keyboard

A comfortable keyboard can make long typing sessions easier. Wireless models can also reduce cable clutter around your workspace.

When choosing one, consider the key layout, size, comfort, battery life and compatibility with your computer.

Best for: Writers, analysts, students and office workers.

Productivity

4. Wireless Mouse

A good mouse can make navigating documents, spreadsheets and creative applications more comfortable than using a laptop trackpad all day.

Look for a shape that feels comfortable in your hand and a sensor that works reliably on your desk surface.

Best for: Everyday computer work and productivity.

Connectivity

5. USB-C Hub

Modern laptops often have fewer physical ports than older computers. A USB-C hub can expand connectivity by providing additional USB ports, display connections, memory-card slots or other interfaces depending on the model.

Before buying one, check which ports your laptop supports and whether you need features such as video output or high-speed data transfer.

Best for: Laptop users with limited ports.

Video Calls

6. External Webcam

A dedicated webcam can be useful if your laptop camera produces poor image quality or you frequently attend online meetings.

Look for good image quality, a suitable field of view and easy compatibility with your preferred meeting applications.

Best for: Remote workers, meetings and online presentations.

Audio

7. Headset or Headphones

A good headset can help you concentrate and make online meetings easier to hear. Models with microphones can also provide clearer communication.

For long work sessions, comfort is often more important than having every possible feature.

Best for: Video meetings, calls and focused work.

Lighting

8. Desk Light

Good lighting can make your workspace more comfortable, especially if you work in the evening or in a room with limited natural light.

A desk lamp with adjustable brightness and direction can help you illuminate your workspace without relying entirely on overhead lighting.

Best for: Evening work and poorly lit desks.

Storage

9. Portable SSD

A portable solid-state drive can provide fast external storage for files, project materials, photos and video content.

It can also be useful for keeping an additional copy of important files, although an external drive should not be your only backup.

Best for: Creators, analysts and anyone handling large files.

Power

10. Surge Protector or Power Strip

A quality power strip can help organize the collection of chargers and devices around a home office.

If you use several electronic devices at your desk, choose a reputable product with appropriate safety features and enough capacity for your equipment.

Best for: Organizing multiple office devices.

Which Gadgets Should You Buy First?

You do not need to purchase everything on this list at once. Start with the equipment that solves your biggest problem.

If You Work Mainly on a Laptop

A laptop stand, external keyboard and wireless mouse are a good starting combination. They allow you to position the laptop screen separately from your hands and can make the workspace more comfortable.

If You Multitask Frequently

An external monitor may provide one of the biggest productivity improvements. It gives you additional screen space for comparing documents, monitoring dashboards or keeping communication tools visible.

If You Attend Many Video Meetings

Prioritize a good headset and, if necessary, an external webcam. Clear audio is particularly important because poor audio can make meetings frustrating even when the video looks good.

If You Work With Large Files

A portable SSD can make transferring and storing large files more convenient. This can be particularly useful for video creators, photographers, developers and data professionals.

How to Build a Simple Home Office Setup

A practical home office does not have to be complicated. A simple setup might include:

  1. Laptop or desktop computer: Your primary work machine.
  2. External monitor: Add more screen space when needed.
  3. Laptop stand: Raise the laptop display to a more comfortable position.
  4. Keyboard and mouse: Use external input devices when working at a desk for extended periods.
  5. Headset: Useful for calls and meetings.
  6. USB-C hub: Add ports if your laptop lacks the connections you need.
  7. Desk light: Improve workspace lighting.
  8. Storage: Use external storage when you need additional capacity.

What to Look for Before Buying

Compatibility

Always check compatibility before purchasing accessories. A USB-C port does not necessarily support every feature available through USB-C, so check your computer's specifications.

Comfort

If you use a gadget for several hours every day, comfort should be a major consideration. This is especially important for keyboards, mice, headphones and laptop stands.

Build Quality

The cheapest product is not always the best value. A slightly better-built accessory may last longer and provide a better everyday experience.

Connectivity

Think about what devices you currently use and what you may add later. A hub with the right ports can prevent you from needing additional adapters.

Warranty and Support

For more expensive electronics, consider the manufacturer's warranty and customer support before buying.

Common Home Office Buying Mistakes

  • Buying too many gadgets: More technology does not automatically mean more productivity.
  • Ignoring compatibility: Check ports, operating-system support and device requirements before purchasing.
  • Choosing solely by price: Extremely cheap accessories may not provide the reliability you need.
  • Ignoring ergonomics: A technically impressive setup can still be uncomfortable.
  • Forgetting cable management: Too many cables can quickly make a workspace messy.
  • Skipping backups: External storage should complement a backup strategy rather than replace it.

Do You Really Need a Second Monitor?

Not everyone needs a second monitor. If you mainly work with one application at a time, a laptop or single monitor may be sufficient.

However, an additional display can be valuable when your work involves comparing documents, monitoring information, researching while writing or keeping communication applications visible.

The best approach is to identify whether switching between windows is actually slowing you down before spending money on another display.

How Much Should You Spend?

There is no universal budget for a home-office setup. Your ideal spending level depends on how many hours you work from home and which tasks you perform.

If you work remotely every day, spending more on the equipment you use constantly can make sense. For occasional home workers, a simple setup may be enough.

Instead of trying to buy the most expensive products, prioritize the accessories that solve your biggest problems.

Our Recommended Beginner Setup

If you are building a home office from scratch, start with the essentials:

  • Laptop or desktop computer
  • External monitor if additional screen space is useful
  • Laptop stand
  • Comfortable keyboard
  • Wireless mouse
  • Headset for meetings
  • USB-C hub if your laptop needs more ports

You can then add a webcam, portable SSD, desk light and other accessories based on your actual needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most useful gadget for working from home?

There is no single answer for everyone. An external monitor, laptop stand, keyboard and mouse can be particularly useful for people who work from a laptop every day.

Is a laptop stand really necessary?

It is not essential, but it can make a laptop-based desk setup more comfortable by raising the screen. If you use one, pairing it with an external keyboard and mouse is usually more practical.

Do I need an expensive home office setup?

No. A useful workspace can be created with relatively simple equipment. Focus on solving your biggest problems rather than buying expensive gadgets simply because they are popular.

Is a USB-C hub worth buying?

It can be very useful if your laptop has limited ports. Before buying, check exactly which connections and features your computer supports.

Should I buy a webcam if my laptop already has one?

Not necessarily. If the built-in camera provides acceptable image quality for your meetings, an external webcam may not be necessary. Consider one if you want better framing or image quality.

Is an external SSD useful for remote work?

It can be useful if you work with large files or need additional storage. Remember that important files should be backed up rather than stored in only one location.

Final Recommendation

The best home-office setup is not necessarily the one with the most gadgets. It is the setup that makes your everyday work easier and more comfortable.

For most laptop-based workers, a laptop stand, external keyboard, wireless mouse and a suitable monitor are a strong starting point. Add a headset, USB-C hub, webcam or external storage when your work actually requires them.

Before buying any gadget, ask one simple question:

Will this solve a real problem in my workspace?

If the answer is yes, it may be worth considering. If not, save your money for something that will.
