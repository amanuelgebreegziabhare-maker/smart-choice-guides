Updated October 2026

A good power bank can keep your phone, tablet, earbuds, or other USB-powered devices running when you are away from a wall outlet. But you do not always need to spend a lot of money to get a useful portable charger.

Why Buy a Power Bank?

Smartphones are increasingly used for navigation, communication, payments, photography, entertainment, and work. Heavy use can drain a battery quickly, especially while travelling or spending a long day away from home.

A power bank provides a portable source of electricity that can recharge compatible devices through USB. The best choice depends on capacity, charging speed, size, ports, portability, and the devices you need to charge.

How Much Power Bank Capacity Do You Need?

Power banks are commonly described by their capacity in milliamp-hours, or mAh. A larger number generally means more stored energy, although the actual number of charges you get depends on the efficiency of the power bank and your device.

5,000mAh

A compact option for emergency charging and everyday carrying. It is useful when you want something small and lightweight.

10,000mAh

A good middle-ground option for many smartphone users. It provides more capacity without becoming as bulky as larger power banks.

20,000mAh

A strong choice for travel, long working days, commuting, and heavy phone use. It provides significantly more stored capacity than compact models.

20,000mAh+

Larger capacity models can be useful when you need multiple charges or want to power several compatible devices during longer trips.

Why Charging Speed Matters

Capacity is not the only specification that matters. Charging speed can determine how quickly your device can recover its battery.

Look at the output wattage of the power bank and compare it with the charging requirements of your device. A higher-wattage power bank does not automatically make every device charge faster because the device itself determines how much power it can accept.

USB-C Power Delivery can be particularly useful for compatible phones, tablets, and other USB-C devices.

USB-C vs USB-A Power Banks

USB-C has become increasingly important because many modern phones, tablets, laptops, headphones, and other devices use USB-C.

USB-A can still be useful for older cables and accessories, so a power bank with both USB-C and USB-A ports may provide greater flexibility.

If most of your devices use USB-C, prioritizing a power bank with a capable USB-C input and output is usually a sensible choice.

Best Power Banks for Travel

A power bank can be especially useful when travelling because you may spend many hours away from a convenient wall outlet.

For travel, consider:

  • Capacity
  • Weight and physical size
  • USB-C charging
  • Charging speed
  • Number of ports
  • Airline and destination restrictions

Always check the current airline rules before travelling with a power bank. Airlines can have specific requirements concerning battery capacity and where power banks can be carried.

What to Look for When Buying a Budget Power Bank

1. Capacity

Choose a capacity based on how often you need to recharge your devices. Bigger is not always better if you mainly need an emergency backup.

2. Charging Speed

Check the maximum output wattage and whether the power bank supports charging standards compatible with your devices.

3. USB-C Support

USB-C can make charging more convenient, particularly if your phone and other devices already use USB-C cables.

4. Portability

A power bank that stays at home because it is too heavy or large is not very useful. Consider how you will carry it every day.

5. Build Quality

Look for established brands and products with clear specifications, safety information, and appropriate warranty or support.

6. Number of Ports

Multiple ports can be useful if you regularly charge more than one device.

Common Power Bank Buying Mistakes

  • Buying the largest capacity automatically: More capacity can also mean greater size and weight.
  • Ignoring charging speed: A large battery does not necessarily mean fast charging.
  • Ignoring compatibility: Check that the power bank supports the charging requirements of your devices.
  • Choosing based only on price: Extremely cheap products may not offer the same build quality, reliability, or support as established brands.
  • Ignoring size: Think about whether the power bank will fit comfortably in your bag or pocket.

Budget vs More Expensive Power Banks

A more expensive power bank is not automatically the right choice. Budget models can be perfectly suitable for basic phone charging.

Spending more may make sense if you need higher charging speeds, greater capacity, USB-C Power Delivery, better build quality, multiple ports, or additional features.

The best value comes from matching the power bank's specifications to your actual needs rather than paying for features you will never use.

Which Power Bank Should You Buy?

For occasional emergency charging, a compact 5,000mAh or 10,000mAh model may be enough.

For everyday commuting and travel, a 10,000mAh or 20,000mAh model offers a useful balance between capacity and portability.

If you regularly spend long periods away from power outlets, a 20,000mAh model with fast USB-C charging can be a better choice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a 20,000mAh power bank worth it?

Yes, especially for travel, commuting, long workdays, and heavy phone users. It provides substantially more capacity than smaller models, although it will also generally be larger and heavier.

How many times can a power bank charge a phone?

It depends on the power bank capacity, the phone battery capacity, and charging efficiency. The advertised capacity should not be treated as the exact amount of energy delivered to the phone.

Is USB-C better for a power bank?

USB-C can be a major advantage because it is widely used by modern devices and can support faster charging on compatible products.

Should I buy a 10,000mAh or 20,000mAh power bank?

Choose 10,000mAh if portability is more important. Choose 20,000mAh if you need more capacity for travel, long days, or multiple charges.

Can I travel with a power bank?

Power banks are commonly subject to airline battery rules. Always check the latest requirements of your airline before travelling.
